# Édouard Philippe | France 2027 research dossier

**Party / movement:** Horizons
**Political family:** Centre-right
**Campaign status (12 November 2026):** Declared candidate; announced on 3 September 2024; continues to reject a right/centre primary proposed by Bruno Retailleau; participated in the 27 August MEDEF debate.
**Ballot status:** Not yet on the official candidate list. A declaration does not confirm ballot qualification.
**Programme status:** The official campaign site publishes an evolving, topic-by-topic programme, marked updated 12 November. The campaign says new proposals will continue to be added.

## Political biography and career
Former prime minister (2017–2020), mayor of Le Havre and founder of Horizons. Philippe declared his intention to run in a Le Point interview on 3 September 2024. His positioning is centre-right and pro-European. Political commitments below are attributed to his statements or published campaign pages; prior government actions remain historical record, not automatically new 2027 pledges.

## Political record
As prime minister he oversaw the early Macron presidency, including labour-market reforms, the Yellow Vest crisis, the start of the COVID-19 pandemic and the initial pension-reform attempt. Those government decisions form part of his record but should not be presented as new 2027 pledges unless he renews them.

## Main policy themes
- Fiscal consolidation and debt reduction
- Work incentives, competitiveness and labour-market reform
- Pension-system sustainability, including a higher retirement age
- Public-service and state reform
- European cooperation, defence and strategic autonomy

## Current, attributable positions
### Pensions | 29 September 2026
**Evidence type:** Public statement
The official programme page, updated 12 November, proposes gradually reaching a legal retirement age of 65 and 45 contribution years in less than ten years for those able to do so. It says one third could retire earlier and maintains health-related exceptions. The campaign also proposes collective capitalization and a €500 initial state contribution to an individual retirement-savings plan. Le Monde attributes a €30bn annual savings estimate to the candidate’s team; that projection has not been independently validated.

### Unemployment insurance | 27 August 2026
**Evidence type:** Debate / campaign statement
In an AFP interview on 27 August, he proposed a maximum 12-month benefit duration for people under 50. This is a campaign proposal, not current law.

### Public finances | 27 August 2026
**Evidence type:** Debate reporting
At the MEDEF debate, he criticized Mélenchon’s debt-cancellation proposal as risking France’s credit standing. The current campaign programme sets a deficit target of about 2% of GDP by the end of the term, a constitutional fiscal rule adopted by referendum, and no new taxes for five years. This is not a balanced-budget commitment.

## Feasibility questions
Key tests: distributional effects of later retirement; whether projected savings are realistic; how a funded pension pillar would be capitalised; and whether spending cuts can be delivered without reducing service quality.

## What remains unanswered
How the pension transition would operate; independent assessment of the €30bn annual savings estimate; implementation of the constitutional fiscal rule; remaining programme themes and detailed costings; outcome of the right/centre candidate-selection dispute.
