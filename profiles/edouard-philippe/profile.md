# Édouard Philippe | France 2027 research dossier

**Party / movement:** Horizons  
**Political family:** Centre-right  
**Campaign status (8 November 2026):** Declared candidate; announced 3 September 2024; participated in the 27 August MEDEF debate.  
**Ballot status:** Declared; not yet officially qualified.  
**Programme status:** No complete 2027 programme was indexed by Observatoire 2027 on 7 November 2026. Earlier prime-ministerial policies are not a current manifesto.

## Political biography and career
Former prime minister (2017–2020), former mayor of Le Havre and founder of Horizons. Philippe announced his presidential candidacy on 3 September 2024, making him an early declared contender. His political positioning is centre-right and pro-European, with an emphasis on governing competence, economic reform and institutional stability.

## Political record
As prime minister he oversaw the early Macron presidency, including labour-market reforms, the Yellow Vest crisis, the start of the COVID-19 pandemic and the initial pension-reform attempt. Those government decisions form part of his record but should not be presented as new 2027 pledges unless he renews them.

## Main policy themes
- Fiscal consolidation and debt reduction
- Work incentives, competitiveness and labour-market reform
- Pension-system sustainability, including a higher retirement age
- Public-service and state reform
- European cooperation, defence and strategic autonomy

## Current, attributable positions
### Pensions | 29 September 2026
**Evidence type:** Public statement
On 29 September 2026 he argued for raising the legal retirement age to 65 in less than ten years, a 45-year contribution period and a funded pillar. The research workbook records a proposed €500 initial retirement-savings contribution and an estimated €30bn annual yield; these are attributed claims requiring independent scrutiny.

### Unemployment insurance | 27 August 2026
**Evidence type:** Debate / campaign statement
At the time of the MEDEF debate he proposed limiting the maximum unemployment-benefit period to 12 months for people under 50, continuing a direction associated with reforms during his premiership.

### Public finances | 27 August 2026
**Evidence type:** Debate reporting
At the MEDEF debate he rejected debt cancellation and emphasised restoring balanced public accounts.

## Feasibility questions
Key tests: distributional effects of later retirement; whether projected savings are realistic; how a funded pension pillar would be capitalised; and whether spending cuts can be delivered without reducing service quality.

## What remains unanswered
A complete 2027 manifesto and costings; how he would assemble a parliamentary majority; details of funded pensions and transition protections.
