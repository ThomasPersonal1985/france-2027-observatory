# Bruno Retailleau | France 2027 research dossier

**Party / movement:** Les Républicains  
**Political family:** Conservative right  
**Campaign status (8 November 2026):** Declared candidate; Les Républicains leader; participated in the 27 August MEDEF debate.  
**Ballot status:** Declared; not yet officially qualified.  
**Programme status:** No complete 2027 programme was indexed by Observatoire 2027 on 7 November 2026.

## Political biography and career
A senior figure in Les Républicains, Bruno Retailleau has served as senator, president of the LR group in the Senate and interior minister. His politics are located on the conservative right, with strong emphasis on authority, immigration control, national identity and public order. He took part in the 27 August 2026 MEDEF debate.

## Political record
His record includes long-standing parliamentary work, leadership of the Senate LR group and a period as interior minister. Statements made while holding office, party positions and new presidential proposals should be separated in the dossier.

## Main policy themes
- Tighter immigration and asylum rules
- Security, policing, criminal justice and state authority
- Fiscal restraint and public-sector reform
- Work, competitiveness and a more business-friendly regulatory environment
- Conservative positions on national identity and republican institutions

## Current, attributable positions
### Pensions | September 2026
**Evidence type:** Public statement
In September 2026 he argued for a minimum retirement age of 63, a full-rate age of 65 and a funded component of around 25–30% for new entrants. These are attributed statements, not a fully costed pension bill.

### Public sector | 27 August 2026
**Evidence type:** Debate reporting
At the MEDEF debate he proposed reducing the number of public servants by 250,000–300,000. The services, timetable, attrition assumptions and implementation mechanism need to be specified before the figure can be assessed.

### Environment / constitution | 27 August 2026
**Evidence type:** Debate reporting
Le Monde’s debate report says he reiterated support for constitutional reform to remove the precautionary principle from the Environmental Charter. The legal scope and implications require close reading of the proposed text.

## Feasibility questions
Key tests: which public functions would lose staff; how a funded pension pillar would be regulated; constitutional and EU-law constraints on immigration proposals; and the consequences of changing environmental constitutional protections.

## What remains unanswered
Full 2027 programme; quantified savings plan; detailed immigration legislation; financing and design of funded pensions.
