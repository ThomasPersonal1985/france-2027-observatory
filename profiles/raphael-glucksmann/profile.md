# Raphaël Glucksmann | France 2027 research dossier

**Party / movement:** Place publique; supported by the PS–Place publique social-democratic primary result
**Political family:** Social democrat / pro-European
**Campaign status (12 November 2026):** Declared candidate (announcement on 23 August); won the social-democratic primary on 11 October.
**Ballot status:** Not officially qualified; the Constitutional Council has not established the final list.
**Programme status:** Six broad axes are listed on the campaign site, which says detailed and quantified proposals will be released progressively. A first-part environmental booklet was published on 9 November; it is not a complete manifesto.

## Candidate status and primary

Glucksmann publicly announced his 2027 candidacy on 23 August 2026. On 11 October, he won the PS–Place publique social-democratic primary with 79,613 votes, reported by Place publique as 66.17%. This result established the primary’s supported candidate; it does not mean the whole left is united behind him or establish official ballot qualification.

## 9 November environmental programme booklet

The campaign’s first-part booklet, prepared with Yannick Jadot, proposes €200 billion in public investment over five years, a one-off €50 billion contribution on the highest wealth, energy and mobility measures, climate adaptation, and an increase in renewable capacity while maintaining nuclear power. The campaign presents this as one part of a programme; further environmental proposals and broader detailed programme material are still forthcoming.

These are candidate and campaign proposals. The published figures do not constitute independent costing, funding-yield verification or assessment of expected climate and household outcomes.

## Other attributable policy positions

### Pensions | 4 October 2026
He has proposed repealing the 2023 reform and using 62 as the legal-age reference, with departures differentiated according to work hardship rather than a uniform age rule. The contribution schedule, hardship criteria, funding and actuarial effects remain unresolved in this review.

### Wages and taxation | 26 August–1 October 2026
He has proposed lowering CSG on workers’ wages and financing the change through a progressive tax on the largest inheritances. In a Le Monde interview he described the target as the top 1% of inheritances, with a progressive scale starting at €2 million. This records the candidate’s proposal; independent revenue and distributional costing remains outstanding.

## Campaign activity

On 6 November, Le Parisien reported a campaign-announced public rally in Paris for 6 December and the launch of a live online question-and-answer format. The rally is scheduled, not yet a completed event.
