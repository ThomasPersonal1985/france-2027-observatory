# Marine Le Pen | France 2027 research dossier

**Party / movement:** Rassemblement National
**Political family:** National-conservative
**Campaign status (12 November 2026):** Declared candidate; stated “I am a candidate” in a TF1 interview on 7 July 2026; participated in the 27 August MEDEF debate.
**Ballot status:** Not on the official candidate list. The appeal judgment and cassation proceedings must be described separately; the Court of Cassation had not issued a final ruling in its 16 October update.
**Programme status:** No complete 2027 programme was linked from the official campaign landing page checked on 12 November. The RN had told an independent transparency project on 6 September that the final programme was forthcoming.

## Political biography and career
Long-time leader of the National Rally (RN), Marine Le Pen has contested the presidential elections of 2012, 2017 and 2022. She declared her 2027 candidacy in a TF1 interview on 7 July 2026. On the same day, the Paris Court of Appeal issued its judgment in the European Parliament assistants case. The Court of Cassation’s 16 October 2026 procedural update reported cassation appeals and a forthcoming QPC hearing; this was not a final ruling on her eligibility.

## Political record
Her record includes leading the RN parliamentary group and serving as an MP; she has repeatedly contested presidential elections. The party has moved from the older Front National brand to the RN and pursued a strategy commonly described as normalisation or dédiabolisation. Her programme should not be inferred wholesale from the 2022 manifesto.

## Main policy themes
- Immigration and border controls; national preference and changes to access to public benefits
- Purchasing power, household taxation and energy costs
- Public order, policing and justice
- National sovereignty and a more restrictive approach to aspects of EU integration
- Industrial protection and economic nationalism

## Current, attributable positions
### Pensions | 27 August 2026
**Evidence type:** Public statement / debate
LCP’s report of the 27 August MEDEF debate says Le Pen supported retirement at 60 for people who began work before age 20, progressing to 42 contribution years and a legal age of 62. The legacy record also contained a €9bn full-year cost estimate. The source checked supports her stated position, but not that amount or its calculation; the estimate remains unverified.

### Public finances | 27 August 2026
**Evidence type:** Debate reporting
At the MEDEF debate she said the RN would present a €125bn public-finance savings plan before the autumn budget debate. This verifies what she said, not whether the amount is achievable or correctly costed. The legacy record’s separate wording about a current-spending deficit rule was not verified in the source checked.

### Economy | 2026
**Evidence type:** Recurring public positions; programme pending
RN themes include reducing production costs and regulations while combining protectionist measures with purchasing-power pledges. Exact 2027 measures and funding should be taken from a published programme, not inferred from party history.

## Feasibility questions
Key tests: whether proposed savings are specified by budget line; compatibility of national-preference measures with French constitutional and EU law; and the interaction between tax cuts, public services and deficit targets.

## What remains unanswered
Any further cassation/QPC rulings and formal ballot qualification; a complete 2027 programme; line-by-line costings for the €125bn savings proposal and the €9bn pension estimate; precise legal design of national-preference proposals.
