# Gabriel Attal | France 2027 research dossier

**Party / movement:** Renaissance
**Political family:** Centre
**Campaign status (13 November 2026):** Declared candidate; announced candidacy on 22 May 2026; leader of Renaissance.
**Ballot status:** Not officially qualified; the Constitutional Council has not established the final list.
**Programme status:** Official campaign site lists 96 initial announced measures and says further measures and the programme will follow. This is a developing proposal catalogue, not a completed manifesto.

## Political biography and career

Former prime minister, former education minister and leader of Renaissance. Gabriel Attal represents the liberal-centrist political family associated with Emmanuel Macron’s governing coalition. He has served as government spokesperson, budget minister, education minister and prime minister. The short premiership in 2024 and government decisions on education, labour and public finances form part of his governing record; they should be distinguished from current campaign commitments.

## Candidate status

Gabriel Attal announced his candidacy in Mur-de-Barrez, Aveyron, on 22 May 2026, as reported by Le Monde. His announced candidacy is distinct from official ballot qualification.

## Programme status and themes

The official campaign site now lists 96 initial announced measures across education, employment, the economy, finance, justice, families, immigration, ecology, energy, technology, democracy, foreign policy, health, defence, simplification and agriculture. It says additional measures will follow and the programme itself will be published when ready. The site’s description does not support calling this a complete manifesto. Campaign proposals are attributed to the candidate; their costs and likely effects are not independently verified here.

The campaign’s recurring themes include economic competitiveness, work and employment, education and school standards, public order, European integration, reform of pensions and public finances.

## Current, attributable positions

### Pensions | August–November 2026

Attal has advocated a universal system governed by contribution duration, with a capitalization component. LCP reports his August proposal as removing the legal retirement age. A Public Sénat summary of the 27 August MEDEF debate instead says he and two other candidates wanted to move the legal age later. His campaign’s 8 November account describes duration replacing age-based steering. The difference remains unresolved pending review of the full MEDEF replay. The previous dossier’s reference to a points system was not supported by the sources checked in this review.

### Families monoparentales | 28 August 2026

LCP reports that Attal proposed excluding received child-support payments from the recipient’s taxable income, creating respite childcare rights for single parents and a single-parent family card. The outlet attributes an approximately €1bn cost estimate to Attal; it has not been independently costed here.

### Artificial intelligence and investment | 2026 campaign measures

The campaign proposes a €200bn, ten-year AI investment plan with public and private financing, and training 20 million employees within three years. These are campaign commitments. The financing, feasibility and expected returns have not been independently assessed in this review.

### Labour costs | 27 August 2026

Secondary debate reporting attributes to Attal a proposal to reverse labour-cost increases introduced since 2024. The precise scope, affected contributions and fiscal implications remain unverified pending review of the primary debate replay.

## Campaign activity and primary proposal

The campaign reports that Attal attended the Rencontres de l’avenir in Saint-Raphaël on 8 November and discussed education, youth, authority, purchasing power, public finances and AI. He reiterated support for bringing the right and centre together through a primary. This remains his proposal; it does not establish agreement from rival candidates or a settled format.

## Polling

It is a secondary average with no disclosed input poll IDs or scenario composition. No new comparable individual poll was verified in this review, so the snapshot is unchanged and is not an election prediction.
