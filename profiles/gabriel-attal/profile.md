# Gabriel Attal | France 2027 research dossier

**Party / movement:** Renaissance  
**Political family:** Centre  
**Campaign status (8 November 2026):** Declared candidate; Renaissance leader; participated in the 27 August MEDEF debate; called for a broad primary of the democrats in late October.  
**Ballot status:** Declared; not yet officially qualified.  
**Programme status:** No complete 2027 programme was indexed by Observatoire 2027 on 7 November 2026. His record in government should be analysed separately.

## Political biography and career
Former prime minister, former education minister and leader of Renaissance. Gabriel Attal represents the liberal-centrist political family associated with Emmanuel Macron’s governing coalition. In the 2026 campaign period he has advocated a broad primary of democratic and pro-European forces, a proposal that is not equivalent to securing the endorsement of other potential contenders.

## Political record
His record includes serving as government spokesperson, budget minister, education minister and prime minister. The short premiership in 2024 and the government’s education, labour and fiscal choices are part of his governing record. Distinguish those decisions from proposals he is making for 2027.

## Main policy themes
- Economic competitiveness, work and employment
- Education and school standards
- Public order and state authority
- European integration and pro-European foreign policy
- Reform of pensions and public finances

## Current, attributable positions
### Pensions | 30 September 2026
**Evidence type:** Public statement
At the 30 September 2026 discussion he advocated moving away from a single legal-age steering model toward a duration-based system with points and a funded component. The cited research says no new legal retirement age or full technical details were specified.

### Business costs | 27 August 2026
**Evidence type:** Debate reporting
At the MEDEF debate he proposed cancelling increases in labour costs introduced since 2024. The precise measures and fiscal consequences require a detailed proposal.

### Political strategy | Late October 2026
**Evidence type:** Campaign statement in project baseline
He called for a broad primary of democratic forces in late October 2026. This is a coalition proposal, not evidence that the other parties have agreed to participate.

## Feasibility questions
Key tests: how a broad primary would be organised and who could participate; the fiscal effect of lower labour costs; and whether pension points and funded components can be reconciled with the existing pay-as-you-go system.

## What remains unanswered
Full manifesto; the scope and rules of the proposed primary; pension-system details; his positioning relative to other centre-right contenders.
