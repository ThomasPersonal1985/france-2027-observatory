# François Ruffin | France 2027 research dossier

**Party / movement:** Debout!  
**Political family:** Left  
**Campaign status (8 November 2026):** Declared contender; has said he is a candidate for the presidency; released 40 proposals in a book on 6 November, while saying it is not a formal programme.  
**Ballot status:** Declared contender; not yet officially qualified.  
**Programme status:** No formal presidential manifesto established in the 7 November snapshot. His book is explicitly not described by him as a programme.

## Political biography and career
Journalist, filmmaker and MP, François Ruffin has built a political profile around working-class living standards, industrial decline, labour conditions and social solidarity. He founded the Debout! movement and has positioned himself as a critic of both the political right and parts of the established left. His November 2026 book sets out proposals but he explicitly says it is not a formal presidential programme.

## Political record
His parliamentary and media work has focused on deindustrialisation, employment, household purchasing power and social inequality. His political relationship with LFI and Mélenchon has been tense, and his strategy for a united left remains a central question.

## Main policy themes
- Reindustrialisation and domestic production
- Wages, worker protections and purchasing power
- Reducing social and territorial inequality
- Ecological transition linked to employment and industrial policy
- A left-wing strategy distinct from Mélenchon’s leadership

## Current, attributable positions
### 40 proposals | 6 November 2026
**Evidence type:** Book / public explanation
His book Au boulot !, released 6 November 2026, contains 40 proposals on industrial, demographic and climate challenges. Ruffin has said it is not a formal programme, so the ideas should be cited as proposals in a book rather than manifesto commitments.

### Alliance strategy | 2026
**Evidence type:** Reported public statement
The project baseline records that Ruffin has said he could withdraw if a viable route to defeating Marine Le Pen emerged, but not in favour of Mélenchon. This should be tracked as a conditional strategic statement, not a withdrawal.

## Feasibility questions
Key tests: which proposals are costed and legislatively actionable; whether industrial policy can create durable jobs; and how a candidacy would relate to a broader left-wing electoral strategy.

## What remains unanswered
Whether he will publish a formal manifesto; whether he remains a candidate if a left-wing unity process develops; and how the 40 proposals are prioritised and funded.
