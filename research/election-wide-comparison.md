## Policy differences documented by new primary evidence

The 2027 edition of LFI’s *L’Avenir en commun* was released on 6 November and presented on 7 November. Its text includes a €1,700 net minimum-wage proposal; retirement at 60 after 40 contribution years; ecological planning; leaving NATO’s integrated command and later the organization; and proposals concerning Palestine, Israel and Ukraine. These are campaign commitments, not independently verified outcomes or costings.

The program proposes a ceasefire and withdrawal of Russian troops from Ukraine, negotiations and collective-security guarantees, while saying any change to borders should be validated by a vote of the populations concerned. The 7 November remarks on a Donbas consultation caused criticism from French opponents and Ukraine’s foreign ministry; LFI and Mélenchon say their position does not endorse invasion or territorial seizure. Keep the written programme, speech, criticism and rebuttal as separate attributed records.

LFI’s Palestine proposals include support for Palestinian sovereignty in territories occupied since 1967, while also considering an equal binational state, and propose sanctions and suspension of the EU–Israel association agreement. Do not shorten this to “ends the two-state solution” without a precise source and attribution; the campaign text itself lists both possibilities.

No other new full programme was verified in the 9–12 November window. A balanced comparison should not fill other candidates’ columns with past-cycle programmes or inferred positions. Existing site policy entries remain in their prior editorial state pending primary-source review.

## Polling comparison — like for like only

The Harris/Toluna poll notice filed with the Commission des sondages on 9 November reports several different first-round candidate configurations. The project retains one record for the “Édouard Philippe sole centrist candidate” configuration, with all 10 listed candidates. Against the previous Harris wave using that same configuration (fieldwork 19–21 October), the notice’s evolution chart shows: Le Pen 35→36; Mélenchon 17→19; Glucksmann 13→12; Philippe 16→14; Retailleau 7→8; Zemmour 5→4. These are small point changes, generally within the notice’s reported uncertainty. The separate “Attal and Philippe both candidates” configuration must not be substituted or averaged as though identical.

The 10 November Harris potential-to-vote study is a distinct question and is not a vote-intention observation. The Vote Public rolling average remains a separate external series; no recalculation was made here.
