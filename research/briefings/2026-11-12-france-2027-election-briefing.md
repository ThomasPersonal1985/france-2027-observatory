## Candidate developments

### Teddy Riner — declared, not ballot-qualified

Riner announced his candidacy on 9 November, which the current Observatory already records. Le Monde reported on 12 November that Macron said he was “very happy” Riner had decided to enter the presidential debate. The same report says people close to Riner denied Élysée orchestration. The Observatory records the public remark and the denial separately; it does not label them a formal endorsement or campaign alliance. LCP’s 10 November report summarises initial policy directions from his 9 November interview: pension reform that retains pay-as-you-go solidarity while not ruling out a funded component; reducing the gross-to-net wage gap; enabling people who choose to work more to increase their income; and reducing and reallocating public spending. LCP says a fuller programme is forthcoming. Attribute these as candidate statements, not a complete or costed platform. He would still need 500 valid elected-official presentations to qualify for the official ballot.

### Jean-Luc Mélenchon — programme release and Ukraine dispute

LFI released the 2027 edition of *L’Avenir en commun* on 6 November and presented it at Le Bourget on 7 November. LFI’s release calls it the **fifth** edition; the candidate campaign site calls the 2027 book the **sixth** edition. The discrepancy is recorded rather than silently resolved. LFI says the programme contains 1,069 measures; the campaign site also displays 1,069.

The campaign programme text includes, among other commitments:

- Retirement at 60 after 40 contribution years and a €1,700 net minimum wage, as reported in contemporaneous programme coverage.
- In foreign policy, calls for a ceasefire in Palestine, Palestinian sovereignty over territories occupied since 1967 while also considering an equal binational state, sanctions against the Israeli government, arms-embargo measures and suspension of the EU–Israel association agreement.
- On Ukraine, a ceasefire, withdrawal of Russian troops, negotiations and collective-security guarantees; it says any agreement changing borders should be validated by a vote of the populations concerned.

After Mélenchon’s Bourget remarks about a possible consultation concerning Donbas, LFI published a clarification on 9 November. It said LFI supports Ukraine’s ability to defend itself, while opposing long-range missile deliveries, foreign combat troops and Ukraine’s EU accession on its stated grounds. Le Monde reported criticism from French political opponents and Ukraine’s foreign ministry, who argued that a referendum under occupation could legitimize annexation. Mélenchon rejected the interpretation that he endorsed invasion or territorial seizure, and said Russia’s invasion was unacceptable. These are contested statements and interpretations; the Observatory attributes them rather than deciding the dispute.

### Éric Zemmour — reaffirmation, not a new declaration

Le Monde reported that Zemmour reiterated on TF1 on 10 November that he intends to run in 2027. His candidacy was already declared in the repository. The new item is a public reaffirmation; the Observatory does not reset his declaration date or imply official ballot qualification.

### Christine Lagarde — potential only

Le Monde’s 6 November report revived speculation about a 2027 role after Lagarde announced an early departure from the ECB. It also said no campaign team was visible and described the possibility as uncertain. In a direct Euronews interview on 9 July, Lagarde said she was not a candidate. She is added to the monitored potential-candidate register because credible reporting has repeatedly discussed the possibility, while her stated denial remains prominent. The Harris survey that included her measured potential to vote for personalities, not a first-round ballot scenario.

## Polling dashboard and tracker

### Corrected Harris first-round record

- **Pollster / sponsor:** Toluna Harris Interactive for M6 and RTL.
- **Notice/publication date:** 9 November 2026.
- **Fieldwork:** 3–5 November 2026; online.
- **Sample:** 2,436 adults representative of the French adult population, including 2,101 registered voters. The displayed scenario uses 1,339 registered voters certain to vote who expressed an intention.
- **Scenario recorded:** Édouard Philippe as the sole centrist candidate. Harris tested several distinct candidate configurations; they must remain separate.
- **Results (% of expressed intentions):** Marine Le Pen 36; Jean-Luc Mélenchon 19; Édouard Philippe 14; Raphaël Glucksmann 12; Bruno Retailleau 8; Éric Zemmour 4; Nathalie Arthaud 1; Fabien Roussel 2; Marine Tondelier 2; Nicolas Dupont-Aignan 2.
- **Comparable previous Harris wave:** the same “Philippe sole centrist” configuration was included on 19–21 October. The official evolution chart shows Le Pen 35→36, Mélenchon 17→19, Glucksmann 13→12, Philippe 16→14, Retailleau 7→8 and Zemmour 5→4. Small differences are within reported uncertainty; do not treat these as a forecast or a decisive trend.
- **Uncertainty:** Harris gives an overall margin-of-error range of ±1.4 to ±3.1 points depending on score; its notice cautions that intentions describe a balance of opinion at a point in time and are not predictive. The individual candidate base is smaller than the full sample.

The project previously dated this poll 5 November, used a secondary archive as its only source, left its scenario unchecked and omitted Arthaud, Roussel and Dupont-Aignan. The same record is corrected rather than duplicated; the prior archive reference is retained and the correction is documented in the data update history. A second transcription error in this briefing and the data record, the upper margin bound written as ±2.9, was corrected to the notice’s ±3.1 on 12 November.

### Separate measure — potential to vote, not vote intention

A Harris survey conducted 27–29 October and filed/published 10 November surveyed 1,041 adults, including 989 registered voters, on whether respondents could imagine voting for each of a list of personalities. The notice explicitly says this measure is **not an intention to vote**. It should not be plotted in the first-round vote-intention chart or merged with a candidate lineup. The project source log records it as a separate polling signal only.
